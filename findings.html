FINDINGS

Patterns observed as the participant dataset grows

This page will summarize recurring patterns found in submitted questionnaire responses. Findings will be presented as exploratory observations rather than diagnostic conclusions or proof of causation.

CURRENT STATUS

Data collection is still developing

The participant dataset is currently being built. Public findings will be added as enough responses become available to make group-level patterns more meaningful.

Until then, this page explains what kinds of findings the project will examine and how those findings will be interpreted.

HOW TO READ THIS PAGE

Findings are exploratory

Questionnaire responses are participant-reported. They are not independently verified medical records.

A participant reporting a professional diagnosis means that the participant states that a healthcare professional diagnosed them. This website does not diagnose or verify that diagnosis.

Differences between groups should be interpreted as patterns within this particular dataset.

CORE MEASURES

Main patterns being examined

The questionnaire was structured so several recurring features can be compared across participants.

PEM onset

Whether symptom worsening begins during activity, immediately afterward, several hours later, or after a longer delay.

PEM severity

How severe participants describe a typical episode of post-exertional malaise.

Recovery

How long the worst period lasts, overall recovery time, and whether participants return to their previous baseline.

Exertion triggers

Physical, cognitive, emotional, and upright activity may be compared as reported triggers.

Symptom combinations

Physical, cognitive, autonomic, sleep-related, and other symptoms can be examined individually and in combination.

Illness onset

Sudden and gradual illness onset may eventually be compared with later symptom and recovery patterns.

PATTERN 03

Symptom clusters

A larger dataset may help identify symptoms that frequently occur together.

Physical

Weakness, heaviness, pain, breathlessness, shakiness, or reduced physical tolerance.

Cognitive

Concentration, memory, processing, word-finding, or mental endurance changes.

Autonomic

Dizziness, heart-rate changes, palpitations, near-fainting, sweating, or temperature regulation problems.

Sleep

Unrefreshing sleep, poor quality, long sleep duration, or changes during PEM.

REPORTING STYLE

How findings will be described

APPROPRIATE

“Among respondents in this dataset, delayed PEM was reported more frequently in Group A than Group B.”

NOT APPROPRIATE

“Delayed PEM proves that a participant has ME/CFS.”

APPROPRIATE

“Muscle heaviness and cognitive difficulty frequently appeared together among these respondents.”

NOT APPROPRIATE

“Muscle heaviness causes cognitive impairment in ME/CFS.”

STARTING CASE

Participant 1 is a starting point, not the conclusion

The project began by examining one detailed participant experience and identifying questions that could be asked more systematically.

Participant 1 is therefore used to generate possible research directions. Patterns observed in one person cannot be assumed to represent ME/CFS generally.

The purpose of the public questionnaire is to determine whether similar patterns appear across additional participants.

LIMITATIONS

Important limitations of future findings

Self-reported data

Questionnaire responses depend on participant memory, interpretation, and personal reporting.

Selection effects

People who choose to participate may differ from the broader ME/CFS population.

Diagnostic differences

Participants may have different diagnostic histories and may have been evaluated using different approaches.

Small subgroups

Some comparisons may contain too few participants to support meaningful interpretation.

Missing information

Participants may leave optional questions unanswered.

Association is not causation

Recurring patterns can generate research questions but cannot by themselves establish biological cause.
