LEARN

Understanding ME/CFS and post-exertional malaise

This section introduces the major concepts discussed throughout this project, including post-exertional malaise, recovery patterns, autonomic symptoms, cognitive changes, sleep problems, and other symptoms that may occur together.

Educational information only

This website is an exploratory educational research project. It does not diagnose ME/CFS, determine whether someone meets diagnostic criteria, or replace evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.

THE CONDITION

What is ME/CFS?

Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome, commonly shortened to ME/CFS, is a complex illness involving more than ordinary tiredness.

People may experience problems involving physical stamina, thinking, sleep, circulation, heart-rate regulation, pain, weakness, sensory tolerance, and recovery after activity.

Symptoms and severity can differ greatly between individuals. The pattern may also change over time.

CORE CONCEPT

What is post-exertional malaise?

Post-exertional malaise, or PEM, describes a worsening of symptoms following exertion.

The triggering activity does not always have to be intense. Depending on the person, physical activity, mental activity, emotional stress, or remaining upright may contribute to symptom worsening.

One reason PEM is important to this project is that worsening may not always happen immediately. Some people describe symptoms appearing during activity, while others report a delay of several hours or longer.

PEM TIMELINE

Activity and recovery may form a pattern

Instead of looking only at whether PEM happens, this project is interested in when different stages occur.

01

Exertion

Physical, cognitive, emotional, or upright activity takes place.

02

Onset

Symptoms may worsen during activity, immediately afterward, or after a delay.

03

Worsening

Physical, cognitive, autonomic, sleep-related, or other symptoms may become more noticeable.

04

Recovery

Recovery may take hours, days, or longer and may differ depending on the episode.

05

Baseline

A person may return to their previous level of functioning, recover only partially, or report a lasting change.

MULTISYSTEM PATTERNS

Symptoms may involve several body systems

The questionnaire separates symptoms into categories so patterns can eventually be compared across participants.

Physical

Examples may include weakness, muscle or limb heaviness, pain, shakiness, breathlessness, and reduced walking or standing tolerance.

Cognitive

Some participants may report memory problems, difficulty concentrating, slower processing, word-finding difficulty, or reduced mental endurance.

Autonomic

Symptoms can include dizziness, heart-rate changes, palpitations, near-fainting, sweating changes, or difficulty regulating temperature.

Sleep

People may describe unrefreshing sleep, poor sleep quality, unusually long sleep periods, or changes in sleep during PEM.

Gastrointestinal and sensory

Other reported experiences may include gastrointestinal symptoms, headaches, migraines, sensory changes, and temperature-related symptoms.

Recovery

Recovery itself is treated as an important pattern rather than simply the absence of symptoms.

AUTONOMIC FUNCTION

Why look at upright activity and POTS?

The project also records autonomic symptoms and whether a participant reports having received a professional POTS diagnosis.

This is useful because symptoms associated with standing or remaining upright may overlap with other activity-related limitations.

The questionnaire keeps a reported POTS diagnosis separate from symptoms such as dizziness, palpitations, fainting, and heart-rate changes.

A participant selecting autonomic symptoms does not mean this website is diagnosing POTS.

RECOVERY

Why recovery is one of the main areas of interest

A central question in this project is not only what happens during exertion, but what happens afterward.

Two people may complete a similar activity yet describe very different recovery patterns.

Questions that may eventually be explored include:

  • How long after activity does symptom worsening begin?
  • When are symptoms at their worst?
  • How long does the worst period last?
  • How long does overall recovery take?
  • Does the person return to their previous baseline?
  • Does recovery change as the illness progresses?

PATTERN-BASED APPROACH

Why collect the same questions from multiple people?

A single person's experience can suggest questions worth investigating, but it cannot show whether the same pattern is common across a larger group.

Using a standardized questionnaire allows responses from different participants to be compared using the same categories.

As the dataset grows, the project may be able to explore whether certain symptom, PEM, recovery, autonomic, sleep, or onset patterns repeatedly appear together.

IMPORTANT LIMITS

What this website does not do

It does not diagnose

Questionnaire answers are not used to decide whether someone has ME/CFS.

It does not prove causation

Two patterns appearing together does not mean that one caused the other.

It does not replace medical care

Medical concerns should be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional.

It does not represent everyone

Responses collected through this site represent the participants who chose to submit them.
