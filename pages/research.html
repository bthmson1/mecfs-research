EXPLORATORY RESEARCH PROJECT

Exploring Post-Exertional Malaise and Possible Biological Patterns in ME/CFS

This project explores patterns surrounding post-exertional malaise and asks whether those patterns may eventually point toward measurable biological changes.

Current Stage: Exploratory Starting Dataset: Participant 1 Goal: Multi-participant analysis

01

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Why this project exists

ME/CFS involves considerably more than simply feeling tired. One of its defining characteristics is post-exertional malaise, or PEM, where symptoms worsen following physical, cognitive, or sometimes emotional exertion.

The purpose of this project is to examine what happens around these episodes and look for patterns that could eventually help identify measurable biological changes.

Important:

The project does not begin with the assumption that one biological system causes ME/CFS.

02

MAIN RESEARCH QUESTION

What measurable changes happen in the body before, during, and after PEM, and do the same patterns appear across multiple people with ME/CFS?

Additional questions

  • Why does PEM begin immediately for some people and later for others?
  • Why does recovery take so long after relatively small amounts of activity?
  • Do autonomic symptoms appear before PEM?
  • Do mental and physical exertion produce similar changes?
  • Does repeated PEM contribute to worsening functional capacity?
  • Are there recognizable subgroups within ME/CFS?
03

CURRENT WORKING HYPOTHESIS

ME/CFS may involve an abnormal response to physical, cognitive, or physiological stress in which one or more systems have difficulty adapting to increased demand and returning to baseline afterward.

This abnormal recovery may contribute to post-exertional malaise.

This is a working hypothesis rather than a conclusion and may change as additional participant data are collected.

OBSERVATIONS

Patterns worth investigating

Several patterns in Participant 1's responses generated questions that could later be compared across participants.

01

PEM Timing

Symptoms may sometimes begin during activity rather than appearing only several hours later.

Research questions

When does PEM begin? Is timing consistent? Are immediate and delayed PEM associated with different symptom patterns?

02

Exertion & Severity

Greater exertion appears to be associated with more severe symptoms and longer recovery.

Research questions

Is there a measurable dose-response relationship between exertion and PEM?

03

Recovery

Recovery may be as important to investigate as the crash itself.

Research questions

When does improvement begin? How long does recovery take? Does recovery become progressively slower?

04

Autonomic Symptoms

Dizziness, heart-rate changes, palpitations and other autonomic symptoms may occur around PEM.

Research questions

Can autonomic symptoms act as early indicators that PEM is developing?

05

Cognitive Exertion

Mental activity may cause worsening similar to physical activity.

Why it matters

This suggests PEM cannot necessarily be explained as a purely muscular phenomenon.

06

Muscle Heaviness

Severe heaviness and weakness may deserve to be tracked separately from general fatigue.

Research questions

When does heaviness begin and does it appear before other PEM symptoms?

07

Sleep

Sleep quantity and quality appear closely connected with symptom severity and recovery.

Research questions

Does sleep change before PEM? Does PEM increase sleep duration? Does poor sleep worsen subsequent episodes?

08

Disease Progression

The event that begins illness may differ from events that cause major worsening later.

Research questions

What initiated illness and what later events substantially changed its severity?

06

POSSIBLE BIOLOGICAL DIRECTIONS

Systems worth investigating

These are possible research directions, not proven causes.

Autonomic Function

Heart rate, blood pressure, circulation, temperature control, digestion and responses to standing.

Blood Flow

Regulation of circulation and delivery of oxygen to muscles, brain and other tissues.

Cellular Metabolism

ATP production, glucose metabolism, fatty-acid metabolism and metabolic recovery.

Mitochondria

Whether energy production or recovery changes abnormally after exertion.

Immune Function

Post-infectious changes, immune signaling and interactions with other biological systems.

Neurological Function

Brain energy use, cerebral blood flow, sensory processing and neuroimmune signaling.

Skeletal Muscle

Muscle metabolism, weakness, heaviness, oxygen use and recovery.

Sleep & Recovery

Changes in sleep quality, duration and the body's ability to return to baseline.

LIMITATIONS

What this research cannot currently show

  • Participant 1 represents one individual.
  • Much of the information is self-reported.
  • Recall may be imperfect.
  • Consumer wearables are not equivalent to research equipment.
  • Website volunteers may not represent the entire ME/CFS population.
  • Other medical conditions may influence symptoms.
  • Correlation does not establish causation.
09

RESEARCH ETHICS

Protecting future participants

Anonymous IDs

Participants can be represented using identifiers such as P001, P002 and P003.

Limited identifying data

Personally identifying information should be minimized.

No diagnosis

Website responses should never be presented as proof that a participant has ME/CFS.

Research oversight

Formal human-subject research may require qualified researchers and institutional or IRB review.

Research and Educational Notice

This project is exploratory and educational. Observations and hypotheses presented here do not establish the cause of ME/CFS and should not be interpreted as medical advice or diagnosis.
