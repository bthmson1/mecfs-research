ABOUT THE PROJECT

Building a clearer picture of post-exertional malaise

This project explores recurring patterns surrounding ME/CFS and post-exertional malaise through participant-reported experiences, structured questions, and future comparison of larger datasets.

PURPOSE

Why this project exists

ME/CFS is a complex illness that can affect physical stamina, cognition, sleep, autonomic function, recovery, and many other areas of daily life.

One of the most important features discussed in this project is post-exertional malaise, commonly shortened to PEM.

Rather than looking only at whether PEM occurs, this project is interested in the sequence surrounding it:

01 Exertion
→
02 Symptom onset
→
03 Worsening
→
04 Recovery
→
05 Baseline

STARTING POINT

Beginning with one detailed case

The project began with a detailed review of one participant's experience.

That first case was used to identify possible recurring patterns and generate questions that could later be asked in a more standardized way.

Participant 1 is not treated as representative of everyone with ME/CFS. One person's experience cannot establish a general pattern.

Instead, the first case serves as a hypothesis-generating starting point.

PROJECT DEVELOPMENT

From one case to a larger dataset

01

Initial case

Review one detailed experience and identify questions worth investigating.

02

Standardized questionnaire

Ask future participants similar questions about PEM, recovery, symptoms, and illness history.

03

Structured database

Store anonymous questionnaire responses so recurring patterns can be compared systematically.

04

Group comparisons

Compare participant-reported groups while avoiding diagnostic or causal conclusions.

05

Future refinement

Improve questions and research directions as more information becomes available.

MAIN QUESTION

What is the project trying to understand?

What measurable changes happen in the body before, during, and after PEM, and do the same patterns appear across multiple people with ME/CFS?

AREAS OF INTEREST

Possible directions being explored

The project does not assume that a single biological system explains ME/CFS. Multiple areas may eventually need to be considered together.

Energy metabolism

Could changes in energy production, energy use, or recovery contribute to post-exertional worsening?

Autonomic function

Could heart-rate regulation, orthostatic intolerance, circulation, or related autonomic changes interact with exertion?

Circulation

Could blood-flow or oxygen-delivery changes contribute to physical or cognitive symptoms?

Immune function

Could immune signaling or infection-related changes influence symptoms or recovery?

Neurological function

Could nervous-system changes contribute to cognitive symptoms, sensory problems, or altered recovery?

Recovery physiology

Could difficulty returning to baseline after exertion provide an important clue about the illness?

PARTICIPATION

How the questionnaire is used

Participants are asked structured questions about:

Diagnostic status Illness onset Possible triggers PEM timing PEM severity Recovery Baseline return Autonomic symptoms Physical symptoms Cognitive symptoms Sleep Existing objective data

Participants may also describe their experience in their own words.

DIAGNOSTIC STATUS

The website does not diagnose participants

The questionnaire records the diagnostic situation that each participant reports.

Possible responses include:

  • A professional ME/CFS diagnosis has been reported.
  • ME/CFS has been suspected by a healthcare professional.
  • The participant is currently being evaluated.
  • The participant self-suspects ME/CFS.
  • The participant is uncertain.

These categories allow future comparisons without treating questionnaire responses as a diagnostic test.

PRIVACY

Protecting participant information

The public questionnaire does not request a participant's name, email address, phone number, street address, or other directly identifying information.

Submitted responses are assigned an anonymous participant code.

Individual responses are stored separately from the public-facing website and are intended to be viewed through the private research administration area.

Public findings are intended to focus on group-level patterns rather than exposing individual participant records.

ETHICAL BOUNDARIES

Important limits of the project

No diagnosis

Questionnaire responses are not used to tell someone whether they have ME/CFS.

No intentional PEM testing

Participants are not asked to intentionally trigger PEM for this questionnaire.

No medical advice

The website is educational and does not replace professional medical care.

No causal claims

Patterns appearing together do not establish that one caused another.

Participant privacy

The project is designed to limit unnecessary identifying information.

Research oversight

If the project develops into formal human-subject research, additional institutional and ethical review may be required before that research is conducted.

LIMITATIONS

What this project cannot establish

Online questionnaire data can be useful for identifying possible patterns, but it has important limitations.

  • Responses are self-reported.
  • Participants may remember events differently.
  • Not every participant will answer every optional question.
  • People who choose to participate may differ from the broader population.
  • Diagnostic histories may differ.
  • Small groups may produce unstable percentages.
  • Questionnaire patterns cannot by themselves prove a biological cause.

LONG-TERM DIRECTION

What the project may become

The long-term goal is to build a larger, standardized participant dataset that can be used to look for reproducible patterns across multiple people.

As participation grows, questions may become more detailed and analysis may examine whether distinct subgroups appear.

Future directions could also include comparisons with objective information such as activity, sleep, heart rate, HRV, blood pressure, laboratory results, POTS testing, sleep studies, or CPET data when such information is already available.

The goal is not to force every participant into one explanation. The project is intended to help identify which questions deserve deeper investigation.

PROJECT PRINCIPLES

The approach behind the project

01

Ask before assuming

Treat observations as questions that need testing rather than conclusions that are already proven.

02

Look for repetition

A recurring pattern across multiple people is more informative than an observation from one individual.

03

Preserve differences

Not every participant is expected to experience the illness in the same way.

04

Separate association from cause

Patterns can point toward research questions without proving why those patterns occur.

05

Protect participants

Avoid unnecessary identifying information and do not ask people to intentionally worsen their health for this questionnaire.

06

Keep refining

Questions and methods should improve as more evidence, responses, and research become available.
